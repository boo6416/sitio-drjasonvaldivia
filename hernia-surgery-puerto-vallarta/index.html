What are the symptoms of a hernia?

Hernias occur when tissue or an organ pushes through a weak point in the abdominal wall. The most common symptoms include:

  • A visible bulge in the abdomen, groin, or near the belly button
  • The bulge becomes more noticeable when standing, coughing, or straining
  • Pain or discomfort in the area, especially when lifting weight
  • A feeling of heaviness or pressure in the abdomen
  • In some cases, the bulge may reduce (disappear) when lying down
  • Sudden intense pain: may indicate a trapped hernia — requires urgent attention

When is surgery necessary?

Unlike other conditions, hernias do not resolve on their own or with medication — only surgery repairs them definitively. Repair is recommended when there are symptoms, when the hernia grows over time, or preventively before a complication such as strangulation occurs (when the tissue becomes trapped and loses circulation, which is an emergency). In consultation, the type, size, and location of your hernia are evaluated to define the best approach.

What is the procedure like?

Hernia repair (hernioplasty) can be performed laparoscopically or openly, depending on the type and size of the hernia. In most cases, a surgical mesh is used to reinforce the abdominal wall and reduce the risk of recurrence.

  1. It is determined whether it is inguinal, umbilical, incisional, or another type, and its size.
  2. Laparoscopic (minimally invasive) or open, depending on the specific case.
  3. In most cases, a surgical mesh is placed to reinforce the weakened area.
  4. The procedure usually lasts between 45 and 90 minutes, with discharge the same day or the next.

Important fact: Hernias do not disappear or shrink with exercise or support belts — these only temporarily relieve discomfort. The only definitive solution is surgical.

What is recovery like?

With the laparoscopic technique, most patients resume light activities within a few days and normal activities, including exercise, in 2 to 4 weeks. It is recommended to avoid lifting heavy objects during the indicated recovery period to allow the mesh to integrate properly.

Frequently asked questions

Is mesh always necessary?

In most cases, yes, since it significantly reduces the risk of the hernia returning. The type of mesh and technique is decided based on each case.

What happens if I don’t get surgery?

Hernias tend to grow over time and the risk of complications (such as trapped tissue) increases. They do not resolve on their own.

How long should I wait to exercise afterward?

Generally, intense exertion should be avoided for 3-4 weeks, but this varies depending on the type of hernia and technique used — you will be given a specific recovery plan.

Can the hernia come back after surgery?

The risk of recurrence is low with the right technique and mesh, though never zero. Following recovery instructions significantly reduces that risk.
